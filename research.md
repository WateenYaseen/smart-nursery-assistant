## 1. Research Objective

The purpose of this research is to investigate existing applications and alternatives that help users identify plants, understand plant problems, access care information, and manage plant records.

The research focuses not only on technical features but also on accessibility, affordability, language support, and ease of use for the intended users.

This is particularly important because the target users may include nursery owners and farmers who prefer Arabic and may have limited experience using digital agricultural applications.

**Research limitation:** Hands-on testing was not conducted due to time constraints. This comparison is based on publicly available product information and research sources. Therefore, it does not claim to represent the complete in-app experience.

## 2. Plantix

**Official website:** https://plantix.net/

**Target Users:**
Farmers and crop growers who need help identifying crop diseases, pests, and other agricultural problems.

**Main Features:**
- Image-based crop problem identification.
- Detection of supported plant diseases and damage.
- Suggested treatments and agricultural information.
- Agricultural knowledge and community features.

**What It Does Well:**
- Focuses on agricultural problems rather than general plant identification.
- Provides image-based crop problem detection.
- Offers agricultural guidance for supported crops and conditions.
- Reports support for multiple languages.

**Limitations and Accessibility Considerations:**
- Its main focus is agricultural crops, so its suitability for ornamental nursery plants and individual potted plants needs further investigation.
- It is not primarily designed around maintaining separate long-term records for every individual potted plant.
- The actual availability and clarity of Arabic guidance should be checked within the application.
- Its suitability for users with limited experience using agricultural applications has not been independently tested.

**Cost:**
The Google Play listing includes advertisements. The available public information reviewed for this research does not establish the complete cost of every feature or any possible paid services.

**Source:**  
https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.peat.GartenBank

## 3. Planti

**Official website:** https://planti.app/

**Target Users:**
People who care for indoor plants, including beginners and experienced plant owners.

**Main Features:**
- AI-based plant identification.
- Plant disease identification.
- Watering and plant-care reminders.
- Plant collections, photos, and notes.
- Shared plant care with family members or roommates.

**What It Does Well:**
- Combines plant identification with care reminders.
- Allows users to organize plant collections.
- Supports saving photos and tracking plant-related information.
- Provides a free tier for basic plant management.

**Limitations and Accessibility Considerations:**
- Its main focus is indoor plants and household plant care rather than nursery operations.
- The free tier has limits, including a maximum of 15 plants according to its official website.
- Additional features, such as unlimited plant records and increased identification checks, require a premium subscription.
- Arabic support within the actual application interface and care guidance needs verification.
- Its suitability for managing a medium-sized nursery with many individual plants has not been established.

**Cost:**
Free to download, with optional premium features. The official website states that the free tier supports up to 15 plants.

**Source:**  
https://planti.app/

## 4. Google Lens

**Official website:** https://lens.google/

**Target Users:**
General smartphone users who want to identify objects, plants, and other things or search for information using images.

**Main Features:**
- Visual search using a camera or existing image.
- Plant and animal recognition.
- Finding visually similar images and related information.
- Searching for information without knowing the exact search terms.

**What It Does Well:**
- Makes visual searching quick and accessible.
- Helps users find plant names and related information.
- Can be used as a general alternative when a user does not know what to search for.
- Supports Arabic-language Google content and localized experiences.

**Limitations and Accessibility Considerations:**
- It is a general visual search tool rather than a specialized agricultural assistant.
- It does not provide a dedicated nursery management workflow.
- It does not automatically maintain structured records for individual plants in a nursery.
- Search results may require the user to open several websites and evaluate the information independently.
- It does not replace a specialized system for tracking individual plant conditions over time.

**Cost:**
Google Lens is available as a general Google search feature. No separate premium subscription is required for the basic visual search functionality described here.

**Source:**  
https://lens.google/

## 5. Accessibility Challenges in Digital Agriculture

Cost, language, and digital familiarity are important considerations when designing agricultural applications.

A research report examining digital agriculture in Jordan, Iraq, and Lebanon identifies several barriers, including:

- Limited awareness and understanding of digital technologies.
- A mismatch between the language used by digital tools and the languages understood by farmers.
- Limited access to financial resources.
- The need for timely, localized, and understandable agricultural information in Arabic or local languages.

These findings support considering accessibility as an important design requirement for the proposed project.

However, they do not establish that every Jordanian farmer faces these difficulties or provide a specific percentage of affected users.

**Source:**  
Harnessing Digital Agriculture in Iraq, Jordan, and Lebanon.  
https://baen-research.agrilife.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2024/05/Harnessing-digital-ag-2021_low-res.Final-ver.pdf

## 6. Identified User Needs and Market Gaps

Based on the product information reviewed and the needs identified during the problem-discovery stage, the following areas deserve further investigation:

### 6.1 Affordability

Some applications provide basic features for free but restrict additional capabilities through subscriptions or in-app purchases.

This may create an accessibility barrier for farmers and nursery owners who cannot afford recurring payments.

The proposed project will prioritize a prototype that does not depend on additional paid services.

### 6.2 Arabic Language and Communication

Agricultural guidance needs to be understandable to its intended users.

An application may provide useful technical features but still be difficult to use if its interface, explanations, or instructions are not accessible to Arabic-speaking users.

The proposed project will prioritize Arabic as its primary language and English as a secondary language. It will also aim to support conversational interaction rather than relying only on technical instructions.

### 6.3 Ease of Use

Some intended users may have limited experience with digital agricultural tools.

The proposed system should therefore focus on a simple workflow: upload a plant image, receive understandable information, ask follow-up questions, and access the plant's record.

### 6.4 Individual Plant Records

Plant identification and general care reminders do not necessarily address the full needs of a nursery owner managing many individual potted plants.

The proposed system aims to maintain a separate record for each individual plant, including its observations and history.

This requirement will be further evaluated against the actual capabilities of existing applications.

## 7. Project Differentiation

The Smart Nursery Assistant is intended to combine:

- Image-based identification of supported plant types.
- Assessment of selected visible plant conditions.
- Conversational plant-care guidance in Arabic.
- Separate persistent records for individual potted plants.
- Photo matching with user confirmation when uncertain.
- A simple workflow designed around small nursery operations.

Its proposed differentiation is not based on claiming that existing applications lack plant identification or disease detection. Instead, it focuses on combining these capabilities with Arabic-first interaction, individual plant tracking, and the practical workflow of a nursery owner.

This differentiation remains a project hypothesis and requires further validation.

## 8. Research Conclusion

Existing applications provide useful features for plant identification, crop problem detection, visual search, and plant care.

However, the proposed project focuses on a more specific use case: helping a small nursery owner manage individual plants and access understandable care information without relying on multiple separate tools.

Affordability, Arabic communication, ease of use, and individual plant records are important design considerations.

The next development stage should investigate suitable datasets, determine which plant types and visible conditions can be supported reliably, and validate whether the proposed workflow is useful to the primary user.
